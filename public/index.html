✦ HISTORY OF AUREBESH ✦

Origins — Behind the Scenes

Aurebesh first appeared as background set decoration in Return of the Jedi (1983), visible on computer screens aboard the second Death Star. At that time, the characters were purely decorative — random shapes with no assigned phonetic values.

Codification by West End Games

In 1993, graphic artist Stephen Crane designed a complete, consistent alphabet for West End Games' Star Wars Miniatures Battles Companion. He assigned each glyph a phonetic value mapped to the Latin alphabet, creating the system fans know today. The name "Aurebesh" comes from the first two letters: Aurek (A) and Besh (B) — mirroring how "alphabet" derives from Greek alpha and beta.

In-Universe History

Within the Star Wars galaxy, Aurebesh is the standard writing system of Galactic Basic — the most widely spoken language. It is seen on starship consoles, cantina signs, Imperial propaganda posters, datacards, and holonet transmissions. The script predates the formation of the Galactic Republic and has remained remarkably stable across millennia.

Structure

The alphabet contains 26 single letters (Aurek through Zerek) plus several digraphs — single glyphs representing two-letter combinations like Cherek (CH), Thesh (TH), and Shen (SH). Numerals use a separate set of symbols, and punctuation marks exist for commas, periods, and other stops.

Prequel & Modern Era

Starting with The Phantom Menace (1999), Lucasfilm began deliberately embedding readable Aurebesh into films. Sharp-eyed fans could decode on-screen text for Easter eggs. The tradition continues through The Clone Wars, Rebels, The Mandalorian, Andor, and the sequel trilogy — where set designers use Aurebesh for everything from wanted posters to droid diagnostics.

Fan & Official Adoption

Aurebesh has become one of the most recognized constructed scripts in popular culture. Official Star Wars merchandise, theme park signage at Galaxy's Edge, and video games like Jedi: Fallen Order all feature functional Aurebesh text. Fan-made fonts are widely available, making it possible to type and read in Aurebesh across digital platforms.

Aurebesh is the writing system used in the Star Wars galaxy.
